## 1. Purpose of This Handbook

This handbook is an internal working document for the GTA Coordinator in the Department of Economics at the University of Warwick. Its purpose is to organise the responsibilities, recurring tasks, communication processes, training arrangements, documents, timelines, and institutional memory associated with the role.

The handbook is primarily designed for the current GTA Coordinator, but it should also be sufficiently clear and structured to support future GTA Coordinators and, where appropriate, departmental leadership.

It is not intended to be a handbook for GTAs themselves, although some sections may be used to prepare materials, emails, presentation slides, induction sessions, or guidance documents addressed to GTAs.

### 1.1 Working Principle

This is a live operational handbook. Where information is based on legacy documents or previous-year slides, it should be treated as **useful but not automatically current**. Items relating to contracts, pay, visa restrictions, workload calculations, online teaching, and named administrative contacts should be checked before being circulated to GTAs.

### 1.2 Status Language Used in This Handbook

To avoid confusing established practice with developing proposals, this handbook uses the following distinctions:

- **Confirmed:** supported by the official role description, current departmental guidance, or direct confirmation from the responsible colleague.
- **Agreed working plan for 2026/27:** agreed in principle for the coming year but still dependent on practical arrangements such as rooms, invitations, payment approval, or timetables.
- **Proposed / to confirm:** an idea that requires further discussion, approval, or governance work before implementation.
- **Longer-term possibility:** a potential future development that is not part of the current role.

Where a proposal concerns pay, contracts, line management, personal data, or the use of developmental records, it must not be presented as settled policy until confirmed by the appropriate departmental or University authority.

## 2. Scope of the GTA Coordinator Role

The GTA Coordinator acts as the main departmental contact point for Graduate Teaching Assistants and coordinates key processes related to GTA induction, administrative training, Economics Network training, GTA forums, and teaching observation.

The official role description defines the purpose of the role as the **main point of contact, guidance, and support for the teaching and professional development of Graduate Teaching Assistants (GTAs)**. The workload model credit for the role is **40 hours**.

### 2.1 Confirmed Responsibilities

The role currently includes:

- Acting as the main contact point for all GTAs in the Department of Economics.
- Organising and/or delivering internal GTA induction.
- Organising and/or delivering administrative training for GTAs.
- Coordinating communication around the Economics Network GTA workshop.
- Coordinating GTA Assessors, including the GTA Coordinator where relevant.
- Coordinating the GTA teaching observation process.
- Redesigning and maintaining the GTA observation form.
- Supporting consistency and clarity in communication with GTAs.
- Signposting GTAs to relevant departmental contacts where issues fall outside the GTA Coordinator's remit.
- Escalating issues to the appropriate departmental lead when required.
- Building institutional memory around GTA training, observation, and developmental feedback.
- Helping facilitate good working conditions, clear expectations, and efficient coordination for GTAs.
- Seeking to align GTA and departmental incentives by making training, observation, and communication practically useful to both.
- Organising and chairing two GTA forums during the year, usually one in Term 1 and one in Term 2.
- Representing the Department at University-wide GTA Coordinator meetings, where relevant.
- Reviewing and updating GTA resources in coordination with professional services colleagues.

### 2.1.1 Official Principal Duties

The formal role description identifies three principal duties:

- Helping GTAs integrate effectively into the Department's teaching and learning environment.
- Providing orientation and ongoing guidance to clarify roles, responsibilities, and professional conduct.
- Supporting academic and professional development through class observations, constructive feedback, and training opportunities.

These formal duties are consistent with the operational role developed in this handbook, but the practical implementation depends on current departmental processes, professional services support, and the annual teaching calendar.

### 2.2 Responsibilities Currently Outside the Role

Based on current understanding, the GTA Coordinator is not responsible for:

- Allocating GTAs to modules.
- Monitoring GTA workload, unless specifically requested or unless issues arise through GTA contact.
- Resolving disputes or substantive issues between GTAs and module leaders.
- Making decisions about GTA contracts, pay, visa rules, or HR matters.
- Providing official interpretation of employment policy, immigration rules, or payroll arrangements.
- Line-managing Teaching Fellows or individuals teaching through other contractual routes such as STP, unless formally confirmed otherwise.

Issues between GTAs and module leaders should be referred to the Deputy Head of Department (Teaching and Learning), currently Claudia Rei. The GTA Coordinator may nevertheless remain the initial contact point for GTAs and should signpost or escalate appropriately.

### 2.3 Potential Longer-Term Development of the Role

A possible medium-term development, perhaps after one to three years of successfully establishing the coordination, training, and observation processes, is for the GTA Coordinator to contribute to matching or allocating GTAs to modules and seminar teaching. This is **not a current responsibility**.

Any future allocation role would need:

- explicit departmental approval;
- coordination with the Director of Undergraduate Studies, PGT/MRes leadership, module leaders, and professional services;
- transparent criteria covering expertise, experience, preferences, timetable constraints, workload, and fairness;
- a clear distinction between developmental feedback and performance-management information; and
- appropriate consent and data-governance arrangements before observation material could inform allocation.

## 4. Role Boundaries and Escalation Principles

Because GTAs may naturally approach the GTA Coordinator as their first point of contact, it is important to distinguish between advice, coordination, signposting, and escalation.

### 4.1 The GTA Coordinator Can Usually Handle

- Questions about induction sessions.
- Questions about administrative training.
- Questions about the Economics Network GTA workshop.
- Questions about the GTA observation process.
- Questions about observation forms, timelines, and expectations.
- Initial signposting when GTAs are unsure whom to contact.
- General procedural clarification.
- Reminders about module meetings, attendance, Moodle, Tabula, Advice and Feedback hours, and professional expectations.
- Collecting feedback from GTAs on recurring issues.
- Coordinating GTA forums and developmental conversations.

### 4.2 The GTA Coordinator Should Usually Signpost or Escalate

- Disputes between GTAs and module leaders: Deputy Head of Department (Teaching and Learning).
- Contract, pay, payroll, or visa questions: professional services / HR / relevant official contact.
- Questions about module-specific content or marking: module leader or Lead Tutor.
- Student welfare or accessibility issues: relevant student support, disability/accessibility lead, programme team, or personal tutor.
- Formal complaints or disciplinary matters: departmental leadership / relevant university process.
- Allocation of GTAs to modules: person or group responsible for allocations.

### 4.3 Recommended Communication Principle

The GTA Coordinator should not feel obliged to solve every issue personally. A useful response structure is:

1. Acknowledge the GTA's concern.
2. Clarify whether the issue falls within the Coordinator's remit.
3. Provide the correct contact or route.
4. Escalate directly where appropriate.
5. Follow up if the issue has wider implications for GTA support or training.

### 6.5 Moodle, Tabula, Forums, and Teaching Materials

GTAs should know where to find module materials and how to use the systems relevant to their teaching.

#### Moodle

- Each module has a Moodle page.
- Module materials, announcements, exercise sheets, and teaching resources are normally stored there.
- Module leaders are responsible for maintaining module Moodle pages.
- GTAs should check whether the module has a discussion forum.
- GTAs should not change module pages unless asked and authorised.

#### Moodle Discussion Forums

- Many modules use Moodle forums for student questions.
- The module leader should explain how the forum is monitored.
- A Lead Tutor may coordinate a rota.
- Encouraging students to use forums can reduce repeated individual emails.
- Forum participation should remain within the GTA's contracted Advice and Feedback responsibilities.

#### Tabula

GTAs may use Tabula to:

- Check allocated classes and student lists.
- Record attendance.
- Communicate with students.
- Access and mark submitted work where relevant.
- Enter marks and feedback where instructed.

GTAs should not manually resolve class allocation issues. Students who do not appear on the class list or request a class change should be referred to the UG/PG Office.

### 6.6 Teaching Hour and Punctuality

The current departmental teaching-hour convention is:

- Teaching begins five minutes past the hour.
- Teaching finishes five minutes to the hour.
- Example: a class timetabled from 10:00 to 11:00 normally runs from 10:05 to 10:55.

This allows students and staff to move between rooms. Previous slides used slightly different finishing times during COVID-era arrangements; those should not be reused.

### 6.7 Support and Feedback Classes

The terms "Support and Feedback classes", "seminars", "classes", and "tutorials" may all refer to compulsory small-group teaching.

Different modules may use different formats. A common structure is:

- Pre-class or prepared questions.
- Unseen in-class questions.
- Small-group work.
- Student presentations or explanations.
- Tutor guidance, recap, and conclusion.

GTAs should follow the module leader's guidance rather than assume that all modules use the same format.

### 6.8 Module Team Meetings

Every GTA should have a module team meeting, or equivalent communication with the module leader / Lead Tutor, before teaching begins.

Topics to confirm:

- Class format.
- Intended learning outcomes.
- Teaching materials and solutions.
- Whether model answers may be distributed.
- Attendance arrangements.
- Moodle forum responsibilities.
- Advice and Feedback hours.
- Marking responsibilities.
- Feedback and moderation expectations.
- Escalation routes.

If the meeting has not occurred, the GTA should contact the module leader. The GTA Coordinator can help signpost, but should not replace module-level support. Carolyn has clarified that attendance at the beginning-of-term module meeting is included within the module's preparation time and is not claimed separately.

### 6.9 Lead Tutors

Potential responsibilities include:

### 6.10 Attendance and Class Allocation

Key messages for GTAs:

- Attendance must be recorded on Tabula by the end of the day in which the class took place.
- Attendance records should be accurate and timely.
- Students should be recorded as present or absent using the relevant departmental procedure.
- The UG/PG Office, not the GTA, decides whether an absence is condoned.
- Students should not switch classes informally.
- A student not listed in the class should be referred to the UG/PG Office.
- If a student disputes an attendance record, refer them to the appropriate office.

Attendance may form part of student monitoring points and can have serious implications, especially for visa holders. This should be explained carefully without asking GTAs to interpret immigration rules.

### 6.11 Advice and Feedback Hours

Advice and Feedback hours are part of GTA teaching responsibilities.

Current guidance indicates:

- Two hours per week for up to two seminar groups.
- An additional two hours for further classes.
- A maximum of four hours per week across modules.
- At least part of this time should be clearly advertised for direct student access.
- Remaining time may include responding to student queries or Moodle discussion forums.

GTAs should:

- Advertise their hours appropriately.
- Explain any booking system.
- Set reasonable boundaries.
- Avoid being continuously available.
- Encourage students to use module forums where appropriate.
- Refer pastoral matters to Personal Tutors or student support routes.

Because workload and payment arrangements may change, the exact figures should be checked before inclusion in annual slides.

### 6.12 Student Communication and Boundaries

GTAs should be reminded that they are often the first person students approach, but they are not responsible for every student issue.

GTAs should normally refer:

- Class switching: UG/PG Office.
- Absence condoning: UG/PG Office.
- Extensions and late submission: relevant official process / UG/PG Office.
- Pastoral concerns: Personal Tutor / student support.
- Assessment complaints: official mark-check or programme process.
- Formal academic integrity concerns: module leader / programme process.
- References: Personal Tutor; GTAs may provide factual information to support a reference if asked.

### 6.13 Absence and Illness

If a GTA cannot teach because of illness or another legitimate emergency, they should:

- Notify the UG/PG Office as soon as possible.
- Inform the module leader.
- Inform the GTA Coordinator where required.
- Complete any required form.
- Where an absence is known in advance, explore an authorised class swap with other tutors and ensure the UG/PG Office is informed.

Current forms and contacts should be confirmed annually.

### 6.14 Assessment, Marking, Feedback, and Academic Integrity

#### Assessment Responsibilities

- Module leaders set assessments and provide marking guidance.
- Module leaders are responsible for moderation and consistent standards.
- GTAs should ask for clarification before marking if criteria are unclear.
- Assessed work is normally submitted electronically through Tabula.
- GTAs should not accept assessed work directly from students.
- Raw marks should be entered unless instructed otherwise; late penalties and extensions are handled centrally.

#### Feedback

Good feedback should:

- Reflect the mark awarded.
- Explain what was done well and why.
- Identify misunderstandings, omissions, or weaknesses.
- Explain how the student can improve.
- Refer to marking criteria where helpful.
- Be specific to the student's work.
- Remain professional and respectful.

Avoid:

- Vague comments such as "good" or "incorrect" without explanation.
- Sarcasm or humour at the student's expense.
- Excessive negative criticism without constructive guidance.
- Generic phrases without identifying what should be expanded or improved.

#### Marking Consistency and Mandatory Training

The working plan is to introduce a short **mandatory departmental marking-standardisation session at the beginning of Term 3 for every GTA undertaking marking**. A likely duration is 30–60 minutes.

The session should cover the Warwick-specific essentials:

- the 20-point scale and when it applies;
- UG and PGT differences;
- aligning comments with marks;
- marking criteria and benchmark answers;
- sample marking and calibration;
- moderation;
- feedback quality;
- common sources of inconsistency;
- what to do when uncertain;
- academic integrity escalation; and
- Tabula and deadline procedures where relevant.

The Coordinator should attend or review the Economics Network Marking and Feedback workshop to understand its content. The Economics Network session is valuable supplementary development, but a departmental session may still be required to ensure consistent Warwick Economics practice.

The general session must not replace module-specific marking meetings. Module leaders or Lead Tutors should hold a marking meeting where GTAs mark an assessment, explain the assessment-specific criteria, and calibrate markers using examples where appropriate.

Before implementation, confirm:

- that attendance is paid;
- who issues invitations and records attendance;
- whether Teaching Fellows or other markers should attend under their own workload arrangements; and
- who leads each part of the session.

#### AI and Academic Integrity

GTAs should not create their own AI rules or tell students categorically that all AI use is prohibited unless this is the confirmed policy for the assessment.

Instead, GTAs should:

- Follow the module leader's instructions.
- Refer students to the assessment brief and official guidance.
- Avoid giving unofficial rulings.
- Escalate suspected misconduct through the proper route.

#### Turnitin

Turnitin is not used in isolation to make a judgement on plagiarism. It provides information on which academic judgement is then made. GTAs should not independently accuse students of plagiarism or academic misconduct; concerns should be escalated through the appropriate module leader and programme process.

This wording should be retained for induction slides because it gives GTAs a safe and consistent way to respond to student questions about Turnitin, similarity scores, AI, and academic integrity.

#### 6.15.1 Current Effectiveness and 2026/27 Consultation

The Forums were originally intended to provide a channel through which GTAs could speak openly, particularly where tensions existed between GTAs and the Department. Recent attendance has reportedly been very low—around one to three attendees from a population of roughly 30 GTAs—so the current whole-group Teams format does not appear to be working effectively.

The first step should be to ask GTAs what they want rather than impose a redesign. Questions should include:

- Do you want the GTA Forums to continue?
- Are they useful in their current form?
- What purpose should they serve?
- What format, timing, or topics would make attendance worthwhile?
- Would you prefer an alternative mechanism such as anonymous questions, a short survey, drop-ins, smaller groups, or themed workshops?
- Even if attendance remains low, do you value retaining the Forum as a channel that can be activated when a collective issue arises?

Possible improvements include:

- collecting anonymous questions before the meeting;
- opening with “What would you change?”;
- using short polls;
- holding smaller or themed discussions;
- separating new and experienced GTA discussion where helpful;
- using an in-person or hybrid format;
- publishing clear actions after the meeting; and
- aligning the Forum with issues GTAs are currently facing.

The official role description currently requires two Forums, one in Term 1 and one in Term 2. They should therefore continue unless the Department formally agrees a different mechanism. Any redesign should preserve a safe route for GTAs to raise concerns and should not turn the Forum into a performance-management exercise.

Before each forum:

- Confirm date, time, room/online format.
- Confirm whether the meeting should be in person, online, or hybrid.
- Invite all current GTAs.
- Invite relevant staff leads depending on agenda items.
- Check upcoming deadlines: marking, module evaluation, exam marking allocation, training sessions.
- Ask GTAs in advance if they have topics or questions.
- Prepare a short update from the GTA Coordinator.
- Bring links to the GTA MS Team, Class Tutors Handbook, GTA webpage, Economics Network events, and any relevant forms.

After each forum:

- Send a short summary and links to GTAs.
- Record action points.
- Escalate recurring issues to the appropriate departmental lead.
- Update the handbook if a new process or recurring issue emerges.

#### 6.15.4 Forum Principles

The forum should be developmental rather than punitive. It should allow GTAs to raise practical issues without feeling that they are being judged. Where sensitive issues arise, the GTA Coordinator should avoid discussing identifiable students or staff in the open forum and instead arrange appropriate follow-up.

## 7. GTA Observation Process

### 7.1 Purpose

Teaching observation is an integral part of GTA development and departmental support.

The process should be described as **GTA teaching observation**, conducted by **GTA Assessors**, with an emphasis on **developmental feedback**.

The observation is not primarily a punitive or disciplinary process. It should support reflection, improvement, confidence, and professional development.

The Department also needs, over time, a coherent developmental picture across two distinct dimensions:

1. **classroom teaching**; and
2. **marking and feedback**.

Some GTAs teach but do not mark, while others may mark without teaching. The two dimensions should therefore be considered separately. The system should recognise strengths, identify support needs, and detect significant outliers without assuming that performance in one dimension predicts performance in the other.

### 7.2 Current / Previous Procedure

Previous guidance indicates:

- The GTA Coordinator matches GTAs with observers.
- Matching should consider subject expertise and fair distribution across academic staff.
- GTAs and Assessors receive process information and the observation form.
- The GTA contacts the Assessor by a set deadline.
- The observation should normally take place by the end of Term 1.
- The GTA informs the Coordinator when the observation has taken place.
- A reflective discussion and feedback meeting should occur.
- Observation reports remain confidential to the GTA and are shared only with explicit permission.

The official role description confirms that the GTA Coordinator is responsible for organising the GTA observation process throughout the year.

### 7.3 Developmental Record

A medium- to long-run aim is to build a developmental history of GTA teaching and, where relevant, marking development.

Potential uses, subject to appropriate consent, confidentiality, and data governance:

- Supporting GTA reflection and professional development.
- Identifying development over time.
- Informing reference letters.
- Supporting Associate Fellowship / HEA applications.
- Identifying training needs.
- Helping the Department understand GTA teaching experience and strengths.
- Potentially informing strategic teaching or marking allocation.

### 7.4 Governance Questions to Resolve

Before observation records are used beyond immediate developmental feedback, clarify:

- Who owns the form?
- Where is it stored?
- Who can access it?
- How long is it retained?
- Is the form shared with the Coordinator?
- Is explicit consent required for reference-letter use?
- Is separate consent required for allocation decisions?
- How can GTAs correct or contextualise records?
- How is confidentiality protected?
- What lawful basis, privacy notice, and retention schedule apply under UK GDPR?
- How can the Department avoid converting a developmental process into undisclosed performance management?

### 7.5 Recommended Observation Workflow

1. Confirm list of GTAs requiring observation.
2. Confirm list of GTA Assessors.
3. Match GTAs and Assessors.
4. Send briefing and observation form.
5. GTA contacts Assessor by deadline.
6. Agree observation date.
7. Conduct observation.
8. Hold feedback/reflection meeting.
9. Complete and share form with GTA.
10. GTA confirms completion to Coordinator.
11. Coordinator records completion status, not necessarily the confidential content.
12. Gather process feedback.
13. Review themes for future training without identifying individuals.

### 7.6 Administrative Tracking Versus Confidential Feedback

Maintain two conceptually separate records:

**Administrative observation record**

- GTA name;
- Assessor;
- module or teaching context;
- agreed date;
- completion status; and
- whether the feedback conversation occurred.

**Confidential developmental material**

- detailed observation comments;
- strengths;
- development areas;
- GTA reflection; and
- agreed actions.

The Coordinator can maintain the administrative record. Access to substantive developmental material must follow the agreed confidentiality and consent framework. No longitudinal database of detailed feedback should be created until data protection, access, retention, and future-use questions have been resolved.

## 8. Redesigning the GTA Observation Form

### 8.1 Design Principles

Before redesign begins, consult the people involved in creating and using the existing form, including Robin Naylor if appropriate, and ask GTAs and Assessors what they would change and why.

The redesigned form should:

- Encourage specific evidence and practical suggestions.

### 9.9 Communication Style for the New Coordinator

Initial communications should be concise, approachable, and honest without undermining confidence. The key message is:

- the Coordinator is learning the detail of the role;
- GTAs should report unclear guidance, practical problems, or suggestions;
- the Coordinator will act where the issue is within remit;
- otherwise, the Coordinator will identify the correct route; and
- feedback will be used to improve future coordination.

Avoid promising that every issue can be resolved. A useful formulation is: “I will do what I can within the constraints of the role, or put you in touch with the person best placed to help.”
